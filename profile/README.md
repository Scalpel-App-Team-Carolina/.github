# Scalpel

**A surgical case log that helps residents see how far they have come, and show it.**

Built by App Team Carolina.

## Why Scalpel

Surgical residency lasts five years. Every year, residents have requirements to meet, and they are expected to take on more of each operation as they go: first watching a step, then assisting, then performing it, and eventually guiding a junior resident through it.

Keeping track of all of that is hard. Cases pile up across rotations, the details of who did what fade fast, and when a resident sits down with their program director, they need a clear record to show they are ready for more.

Scalpel gives them that record. Residents log each case right after surgery, step by step, and the app turns those logs into a clear picture of their experience: what they have done, how involved they were, and whether they are on track for the year.

## What residents can do

**Find the procedure.** Browse by rotation (Colorectal, Trauma, Vascular, and so on) to reach a procedure. Each procedure page shows how many times you have logged it, when you last did it, its surgical steps in order, what is expected of a resident at each training year, and background on the condition with links to study resources.

**Log a case.** Record the date, the surgical team, and your role on every step of the operation: Observed, Assisted, Primary Surgeon, or Supervised Junior. Add an overall role and personal notes. The entry saves as a draft from the first tap, so an interrupted log can be finished later.

**Look back.** Every case lives in a history list, newest first. Search by procedure name, then open any case to see each step, your role in it, and your notes.

**See your progress.** A dashboard shows total cases, how many times you have done each procedure, how often you observed, assisted, operated, or supervised, and how your experience has grown from PGY1 (first postgraduate year) through Chief year. It shows where you stand against your yearly requirements and highlights procedures and steps where you have little experience, so you know what to prioritize.

**Bring it to the meeting.** Export your case log as a spreadsheet to share with your program director.

## Why step by step

A case count only says a resident was in the room. Scalpel records which parts of the operation they watched, assisted with, or performed themselves, so their growth toward operating independently shows up in the data.

## Private by design

- No patient information. Scalpel never asks for it, and the notes field reminds residents to leave it out.
- Attendings are recorded by initials and role only.
- Logs are stored on the resident's own phone and belong to them. Program directors only see what a resident chooses to export.

## Where we are

Scalpel starts with first-year general surgery residents, with appendectomy as the pilot procedure. Procedure content, including the steps and what is expected each year, comes from medical students on our clinical team.

Coming next: search and recent procedures for faster logging, filtering by training year, formatted Excel reports, and backup across devices.
